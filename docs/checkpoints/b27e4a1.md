# Workflow checkpoint — persistent barycentric surface anchors

Base remote revision: `5e4d1ae6e7a0ae3268612ab9f009826949843f03`.
Implementation revision: `b27e4a1167dbd7495c77283c6aab052de2f0f4f8`.
Capability: bind an independently editable component to one exact authored spot on an indexed support, then preserve that face/barycentric correspondence through same-topology form edits without re-running a nearest-surface search.

## Accepted change

`src/lib/surface-mount.js` now distinguishes two useful attachment semantics. Existing `surfaceMount()` remains search-based intent: on each rebuild it finds the nearest admissible surface point again. New `bindSurfaceAnchor()` converts a chosen mount into JSON-safe persistent data containing one indexed triangle, its three vertex indices, barycentric point, affine tangent weights, normal mode, clearance, and the same independent component-local transform. `resolveSurfaceAnchor()` / `attachSurfaceAnchor()` rebuild the component frame from the current positions and normals of those exact vertices.

The anchor also records the original face corner triplet as a topology guard. If the recorded face vanishes or its corner indices change, resolution fails with an explicit rebind requirement rather than silently jumping to a new surface. Barycentric position and tangent transport are support-local construction data; they are not glTF semantics or a universal remeshing correspondence.

## Research used

See `docs/research/surface-anchors.md`.

- Blender Surface Deform documents an explicit Bind step whose stored relation then follows later target-mesh deformation. Adaptation: separate one-time binding from repeated nearest-surface search, while limiting this repository helper to one rigid component frame on one triangle rather than implementing Blender's multi-vertex deformation modifier.
- Three.js r186 `Triangle` documents barycentric coordinates and interpolation helpers. Adaptation: store `{triangleIndex, indices, barycoord}` and reconstruct the point from the same three current support vertices. The recorded corner triplet is also used as a strict topology check.
- Repository-specific frame adaptation: express the resolved tangent at bind time as affine weights over the triangle vertices (weights sum to zero), then evaluate and re-project that direction after the support deforms. This preserves authored roll better than freezing a support-local tangent vector.

## Ownership and limitations

Persistent anchors are deliberately a same-indexed-topology contract. They do not survive arbitrary face reordering, subdivision, decimation, booleans, remeshing, or unrelated reconstruction unless a future topology constructor supplies an exact anchor remap. The helper binds a rigid component frame, not every vertex of a deforming mesh; it does not solve collisions, dependency scheduling, rig parenting, inside/outside classification, or export persistent anchor semantics. Smooth-normal mode can also change orientation when authored vertex normals change substantially even though positional correspondence remains exact.

## Rejected approach

Do not keep using nearest-surface re-query when the author means “this exact spot.” That behavior is useful and remains available, but the study demonstrates that it can legitimately switch triangles under large shape changes. Conversely, do not guess a replacement face after topology changes: v1 fails and requires an explicit rebind instead of fabricating correspondence.

## Next workflow priority

Add an exact anchor-remap contract to one topology constructor that already owns face/corner provenance—most usefully solidification's outer/inner copies—so an authored anchor can intentionally follow a known topology change rather than being limited to same-topology form edits. Keep nearest-surface rebind as the explicit fallback for unrelated geometry.
